When Prices Embed Logistics

Prices do not move commodities.
Systems do.

What we observe in LNG and oil markets is not just price fluctuation. It is the manifestation of constraints embedded in global infrastructure.

Using UN Comtrade data, I constructed a simple CIF–FOB unit value proxy. This is not a freight rate. It is a structural signal — capturing logistics pressure embedded in trade values.

The pattern is unmistakable:

  • 2020: Collapse — demand shock
  • 2022: Spike — system under stress
  • 2023–2024: Normalization — partial rebalancing

But the critical insight lies in the divergence between LNG and oil.

Oil flows through a flexible system. LNG does not.

LNG is constrained by liquefaction capacity, specialized vessels, receiving terminals, and long-term contracts. These are not market variables. They are physical structures.

This means substitution between LNG and oil is not simply a function of price.

It is a function of what the system allows.

When LNG prices rise, oil may appear as an alternative. But unless infrastructure, logistics, and industrial processes align, substitution cannot occur at scale.

This is where most market analysis stops too early.

Prices tell us what changed.
Flows explain why.
Structures define what is possible.

Commodity Flow is built on this premise.

Not to describe markets — but to read the systems behind them.
